# Temperature Relaxation — End-to-End Case

Verifies that the production solver correctly integrates the thermal ODE under
pure conduction ($\mathrm{Nu}=2$) by comparing 25 particle trajectories against the
closed-form $T(x)$ solution.

---

## Purpose

The test decouples heat from drag by injecting at the gas velocity ($k_V = 1$),
zeroing axial slip and hence $\mathrm{Re}$ along the entire trajectory.  With
$\mathrm{Re} = 0$ the Ranz–Marshall correlation reduces to $\mathrm{Nu} = 2$ — the
textbook stagnant-sphere conduction value (not taken from the code).  This pins the
thermal ODE assembly in `Lib_Equations.f90`, the heat path in `Lib_RHS.f90`, and
the integrator against an independent analytic oracle, with no drag coupling
introducing approximation error.

---

## Oracle

With $k_V = 1$ the axial slip is zero everywhere, so $\mathrm{Re} = 0$ and
Ranz–Marshall gives $\mathrm{Nu} = 2$.  The thermal ODE is then linear:

$$
\frac{dT_p}{dt} = \frac{T_g - T_p}{\tau_T}, \qquad
\tau_T = \frac{c_p\,\rho_p\,d^2}{12\,k_g}
$$

With $x = u_g t$ (particle coasts at $u_g$) the solution can be written as a
**time-independent** $x \to T$ relation anchored at the first recorded point
$(x_a, T_a)$ of each particle:

$$
T_p(x) = T_g + (T_a - T_g)\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{x - x_a}{L}\right), \qquad
L = u_g\,\tau_T
$$

This eliminates time from the comparison, so it is unaffected by the ODE solver's
internal step-size choices.

For the case parameters:
$\tau_T = c_p\,\rho_p\,d^2/(12\,k_g) \approx 1.670 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s}$,
$L = u_g\,\tau_T \approx 1.670 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}$.

---

## Tolerance

The trajectory file uses format F12.6; each $x$ and $T$ value carries a half-ULP
truncation $\delta = 0.5 \times 10^{-6}$.  The residual
$r = T_\mathrm{meas} - T_\mathrm{pred}(x_\mathrm{meas})$ collects four such
truncations via the partial derivatives at the test point and at the anchor:

$$
\mathrm{tol}(x) = \delta\,\bigl(2 + 2\,|T_\mathrm{meas} - T_g|/L\bigr) + 10^{-8}
$$

The $10^{-8}$ floor covers the ODE integrator ($\mathrm{rtol} = \mathrm{atol} =
10^{-11}$ puts SDIRK4 roughly three orders below the F12.6 floor; non-binding).
The check is restricted to the resolved window $|T_g - T_\mathrm{meas}| > 1\,\mathrm{K}$;
near saturation ($T_p \to T_g$) the F12.6 floor cannot discriminate the exponential
tail — not a code defect, so correctly excluded.

!!! note "Non-vacuous gate"
    At least $N_\mathrm{good} = 20$ particles must pass the windowed check.  This
    guards against a trivially passing case in which the thermal transient is too
    short to sample at all.
