## Purpose

This case validates the full evaporation-to-trajectory path: the `evaporation()`
function, its coupling in `Lib_RHS.f90`, the diameter update in the ODE state
vector, and the resulting $d^2(x)$ trajectory against an independent oracle.

## Oracle

Under pure d²-law evaporation (stagnant film, $\mathrm{Nu} = 2$) the diameter
squared decays linearly in time:

$$
d^2(t) = d_0^2 - K t, \qquad
K = \frac{8\,k_g}{\rho_l\,c_{pg}}\ln(1 + B_T)
$$

(Godsave 1953; Spalding 1953; `[Lef89]`; factor 8 confirmed independently by
$\dot{m} = 2\pi d\,(k_g/c_{pg})\ln(1+B_T)$ at $\mathrm{Nu}=2$).  With
$x = u_g t$ this gives a closed-form $d^2(x)$ along the trajectory.

The oracle (`check.py`) integrates the rate kernel along the *measured*
$T_p(x)$, making it independent of the thermal sub-model.
